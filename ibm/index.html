About this experiment

Each individual gives birth at the selected rate b (initially 3.05). A newborn is placed at a Gaussian displacement with standard deviation s in each direction. The background death rate is d = 0.1b. Pairwise competition adds the kernel contributions of the other individuals to this rate.

Kp(r) = exp[−(r / rc)p] / Zp

The scale rc sets the distance at which the kernel falls to 1/e of its peak. The profile is super-Gaussian at p > 2 and approaches a disk as p increases. The kernel integrates to one over the periodic domain and uses shortest periodic distances. The plot shows its shape relative to its peak.

Higher-order competition adds a local death rate to the pairwise contribution.

dHOI,i = γ [mi / (π rh.o.2)]2

Here mi counts other individuals within rh.o. of individual i across the periodic domain. Each neighbour can contribute to both factors of the square. The slider sets bγ, so at a fixed slider value γ varies inversely with b. At zero, only pairwise competition contributes.

Physica D · Patterns uses rc = 2.2, s = 0.15 and b = 3.05 from the article’s ordered cluster example, with HOI off. Both presets approximate the published disk-shaped pairwise kernel with the same steep super-Gaussian, p = 128.

HOI · browser uses the study’s rc = 1.25, rh.o. = 0.0625 and s = 0.05, with b = 50 and bγ = 0.025 for exploration at lower population. Figure 1 of the preprint uses b = 1920. The pattern regimes at the browser settings need separate validation.

Reset starts with 1,098 random individuals at your selected rates. At zero HOI, increasing b raises the homogeneous population scale, 400(b − d). Larger populations can advance more slowly.
